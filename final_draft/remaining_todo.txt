What last year's G8 notebook did better than us

They made the assessment notebook feel more complete as a presentation object.
There are more visible plots, more intermediate explanations, and more final
performance measures.

They also included a wider set of statistics:
- Sharpe ratio
- Sortino ratio
- maximum drawdown
- Value at Risk
- buy-and-hold comparison

Our statistics are cleaner and more consistent, but theirs looks broader at
first glance. This does not mean we should copy everything, but it shows why
presentation polish matters.

They also showed the individual signals visually. For example, they plotted
price series together with buy/sell markers or indicator bands. We now have
similar signal-activity plots, which helps the audience see what the strategies
are actually doing.


Ideas worth taking from them

1. Keep the signal-specific plots.
   These make the assessment notebook easier to present because we can point to
   when each signal is active instead of only explaining code.

2. Consider one broader statistics table only if there is time.
   We already have annualized return, volatility, Sharpe ratio and drawdown.
   Sortino ratio or Value at Risk could be added, but only if implemented
   cleanly in NumPy and explained briefly.

3. Keep our differentiation clear.
   We should not copy their MACD/RSI/Bollinger approach. Our advantage is that
   our signals are economically motivated: volatility regime, oil/energy
   conditions and gold safe-haven exposure.
